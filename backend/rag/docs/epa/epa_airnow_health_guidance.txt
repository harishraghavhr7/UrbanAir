=== SECTION 1: US AQI SCALE AND HEALTH CATEGORIES ===

The Air Quality Index (AQI) is EPA's index for reporting daily air quality.
It tells how clean or polluted the air is and what associated health effects might be.

AQI Scale:
| AQI Value | Category | Color | Who is Affected |
|-----------|----------|-------|-----------------|
| 0–50 | Good | Green | Air quality is satisfactory; little or no risk |
| 51–100 | Moderate | Yellow | Acceptable; unusually sensitive may have minor symptoms |
| 101–150 | Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups | Orange | Sensitive groups at risk; general public less likely affected |
| 151–200 | Unhealthy | Red | Everyone may begin experiencing effects |
| 201–300 | Very Unhealthy | Purple | Health alert; serious effects for everyone |
| 301–500 | Hazardous | Maroon | Emergency conditions; affects entire population |

AQI is calculated for five major pollutants regulated under the Clean Air Act:
- Ground-level ozone (O3)
- Particle pollution / PM2.5
- Particle pollution / PM10
- Carbon monoxide (CO)
- Sulfur dioxide (SO2)
- Nitrogen dioxide (NO2)

=== SECTION 2: PM2.5 HEALTH GUIDANCE BY POPULATION ===

SENSITIVE GROUPS (AQI 101–150, PM2.5 35.5–55.4 µg/m³):
Who is a sensitive group:
- People with lung disease including asthma
- People with heart disease or who have had a stroke
- Children under 18 (especially under 5)
- Adults 65 years and older
- People who are active outdoors (athletes, outdoor workers)
- People with diabetes
- People with certain genetic variants that affect pollution processing

Recommended actions for sensitive groups at AQI 101–150:
- Reduce prolonged or heavy exertion outdoors
- Take more breaks during outdoor activities
- Watch for symptoms such as coughing, shortness of breath, chest tightness
- Have asthma action plan ready; keep reliever medication on hand
- Consider moving outdoor activities inside or rescheduling

UNHEALTHY for everyone (AQI 151–200, PM2.5 55.5–150.4 µg/m³):
- Sensitive groups: Avoid prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion; move activities indoors
- Everyone else: Reduce prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion
- If symptoms develop (coughing, difficulty breathing): go indoors; call doctor if worsening
- EPA recommendation: Run air purifiers indoors; keep windows closed

VERY UNHEALTHY (AQI 201–300, PM2.5 150.5–250.4 µg/m³):
- Sensitive groups: Avoid ALL outdoor physical activity; remain indoors
- Everyone: Avoid prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion; consider staying indoors
- Keep indoor air clean: run HEPA air purifier, do not vacuum (stirs up particles), avoid candles/incense
- If you must go outside: wear a well-fitted N95 respirator

HAZARDOUS (AQI 301–500, PM2.5 > 250.4 µg/m³):
- Everyone: Avoid all outdoor physical activity
- Sensitive groups: Remain indoors; go to a clean-air shelter if home lacks air purification
- Keep all windows and doors closed; seal gaps with towels or weather stripping
- Do not rely on ceiling fans — they circulate air but do not filter it
- Seek emergency medical care for: chest pain, severe shortness of breath, heart attack symptoms

=== SECTION 3: PROTECTING CHILDREN FROM AIR POLLUTION (EPA GUIDANCE) ===

Why children are more vulnerable:
- Children's lungs are still developing through age 18
- Children breathe more air relative to their body size than adults
- Children spend more time being physically active outdoors
- Children's immune and respiratory defense systems are less developed
- Air pollution during childhood is linked to: reduced lung function, asthma development, cardiovascular disease risk in adulthood

EPA recommendations for children's air quality protection:
- Check AQI before planning outdoor activities for children
- Use AirNow.gov or EPA's AirNow app for real-time AQI
- When AQI > 100: limit time and intensity of children's outdoor activities
- When AQI > 150: keep children indoors; cancel outdoor sports, recess, school activities
- Schools: consider indoor recess policies when AQI is elevated
- For asthmatic children: follow asthma action plan; ensure school nurse has medication
- Child-sized N95 respirators are available but require proper fitting
- Create a clean room: one room in the house with air purifier running, where children spend most time during high-pollution events

Asthma and air pollution — children:
- Air pollution is a leading trigger of asthma attacks in children
- PM2.5, O3, NO2, and cold air are all triggers
- Asthma is the leading cause of school absenteeism from chronic illness in the US
- During high-AQI events, asthma emergency room visits increase by 10-30%
- Well-controlled asthma with proper medication significantly reduces risk during pollution events

=== SECTION 4: PM2.5 TECHNICAL STANDARD (NAAQS) ===

The National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS) for PM2.5 were revised in 2024:
- Annual standard: 9 µg/m³ (tightened from 12 µg/m³ in 2024)
- 24-hour standard: 35 µg/m³ (unchanged)

These are legally enforceable standards. Areas exceeding them are classified as "nonattainment."

For comparison:
- 24-hour average PM2.5 of 55 µg/m³ = AQI 151 (Unhealthy)
- 24-hour average PM2.5 of 150 µg/m³ = AQI 200 (top of Unhealthy)
- 24-hour average PM2.5 of 250 µg/m³ = AQI 300 (top of Very Unhealthy)

=== SECTION 5: OZONE (O3) HEALTH GUIDANCE ===

Ground-level ozone is formed when sunlight reacts with NOx and VOCs (from vehicle exhaust, industrial emissions).
Ozone season: typically April–October in Northern Hemisphere; peaks on hot, sunny, calm days.

Ozone AQI breakpoints:
| AQI | O3 (8-hr, ppm) |
|-----|----------------|
| 0–50 | 0–0.054 |
| 51–100 | 0.055–0.070 |
| 101–150 | 0.071–0.085 |
| 151–200 | 0.086–0.105 |
| 201–300 | 0.106–0.200 |

Ozone health effects:
- Inflames and damages airways, similar to sunburn on lung tissue
- Reduces lung function even in healthy people
- Aggravates asthma and other lung diseases
- Causes chest pain, coughing, throat irritation, congestion
- Long-term: may cause permanent scarring of lung tissue

Protection from ozone:
- Ozone is highest on hot, sunny afternoons (2 PM–7 PM) — plan outdoor activities for morning or evening
- Unlike PM2.5, ozone is a gas; N95 masks do NOT filter it effectively
- Ozone penetrates indoors: 50-80% of outdoor ozone reaches indoors; air purifiers with activated carbon help
- Stay in air-conditioned spaces during high-ozone days
