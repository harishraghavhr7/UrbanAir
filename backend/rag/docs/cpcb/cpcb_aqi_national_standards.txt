=== SECTION 1: INDIA NATIONAL AQI FRAMEWORK ===

India's National AQI was launched in October 2014 under the Swachh Bharat Mission.
It is computed from 8 pollutants (unlike the US AQI which primarily uses PM2.5 and PM10):
1. PM2.5
2. PM10
3. Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2)
4. Sulfur Dioxide (SO2)
5. Carbon Monoxide (CO)
6. Ozone (O3)
7. Ammonia (NH3)
8. Lead (Pb)

The AQI is calculated as the maximum sub-index across all measured pollutants.
Only pollutants with at least 16 hours of data in a 24-hour period are included.

AQI Categories and Color Codes:
| AQI Range | Category      | Color  |
|-----------|---------------|--------|
| 0–50      | Good          | Green  |
| 51–100    | Satisfactory  | Light green |
| 101–200   | Moderate      | Yellow |
| 201–300   | Poor          | Orange |
| 301–400   | Very Poor     | Red    |
| 401–500   | Severe        | Dark red/maroon |

NOTE: India's "Satisfactory" (51-100) is equivalent to US AQI "Moderate". India's "Moderate" (101-200) overlaps US "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" and "Unhealthy".

PM2.5 Breakpoints for India National AQI:
| AQI  | PM2.5 (µg/m³) | Health Implications |
|------|----------------|---------------------|
| 0–50 | 0–30           | Minimal Impact |
| 51–100 | 31–60        | Minor breathing discomfort to sensitive people |
| 101–200 | 61–90       | Breathing discomfort to people with lung/heart disease |
| 201–300 | 91–120      | Breathing discomfort to most people on prolonged exposure |
| 301–400 | 121–250     | Respiratory illness to people on prolonged exposure |
| 401–500 | 250+        | Affects healthy people; serious risk to sensitive groups |

PM10 Breakpoints:
| AQI  | PM10 (µg/m³) |
|------|--------------|
| 0–50 | 0–50         |
| 51–100 | 51–100     |
| 101–200 | 101–250   |
| 201–300 | 251–350   |
| 301–400 | 351–430   |
| 401–500 | 430+       |

=== SECTION 2: HEALTH IMPACTS BY AQI CATEGORY (CPCB) ===

Good (0–50):
- Minimal impact. Air quality is satisfactory and poses little or no health risk.
- Recommended actions: Enjoy outdoor activities freely.

Satisfactory (51–100):
- Minor breathing discomfort to people with lung disease such as asthma.
- Sensitive groups: Consider reducing prolonged outdoor exertion.
- General population: Can engage in usual outdoor activities.

Moderate (101–200):
- Breathing discomfort to people with lung and heart diseases, children and older adults.
- People with respiratory or heart disease, the elderly and children should limit prolonged outdoor exertion.
- General population: Unusually sensitive individuals may experience symptoms.

Poor (201–300):
- Breathing discomfort to most people on prolonged exposure.
- People with lung diseases such as asthma should avoid outdoor exertion.
- General population: Sensitive groups should limit prolonged outdoor exertion.

Very Poor (301–400):
- Respiratory illness on prolonged exposure.
- Avoid outdoor activities. People with heart or lung disease, elderly, and children should remain indoors.
- General population: Avoid prolonged outdoor exertion; wear N95 mask if going out.

Severe (401–500):
- Affects healthy people and seriously compromises the health of those with lung/heart disease.
- All people: Avoid outdoor activities entirely. Schools and outdoor events should be cancelled.
- Emergency health measures: Use air purifiers, keep windows shut, seek medical attention for symptoms.

=== SECTION 3: GRADED RESPONSE ACTION PLAN (GRAP) — 2023 ===

GRAP is implemented by the Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) in NCR (National Capital Region).
It triggers progressively stricter actions as AQI worsens.

GRAP Stage I — Poor AQI (201–300):
- Ban on use of coal and firewood in hotels, restaurants, open eateries
- Mechanized sweeping of roads with water sprinkling
- Citizens advised to use public transport; carpool encouraged
- Ban on garbage burning

GRAP Stage II — Very Poor AQI (301–400):
- Ban on diesel generator sets (except hospitals, emergency services)
- Strict enforcement of PUC (Pollution Under Control) norms
- Construction activities restricted — dust control measures mandatory
- Citizens advised: avoid private vehicles, work from home if possible
- Schools may be closed for outdoor activities

GRAP Stage III — Severe AQI (401–450):
- BS III petrol and BS IV diesel four-wheelers banned in Delhi
- Mining activities stopped
- Schools advised to close or shift to online mode for children up to Class 5
- Night construction banned
- Citizens: avoid all non-essential outdoor trips

GRAP Stage IV — Severe+ AQI (>450):
- Trucks (non-essential) entry into Delhi banned
- All construction and demolition activities stopped
- Schools up to Class 12 shifted to online mode
- State government may impose odd-even vehicle scheme
- Industries not running on clean fuels shut down

=== SECTION 4: INDIA-SPECIFIC POLLUTION SOURCES AND SEASONALITY ===

Winter Smog — October to February (Worst Season):
Primary causes in North India:
1. Stubble burning: Punjab and Haryana farmers burn crop residue post-harvest (October-November)
   - Contributes 20-40% of Delhi's PM2.5 during peak burning season
   - Fires visible on satellite imagery; smoke travels 300-500 km to Delhi NCR
2. Temperature inversion: Cold surface air traps pollutants under a warm air cap
   - Inversion height drops to 100-200 m in winter (vs 1000-2000 m in summer)
   - Pollutants accumulate near ground; no vertical mixing possible
3. Diwali firecrackers: Single-night PM2.5 spike of 500-1000 µg/m³ in Delhi (October/November)
4. Vehicle emissions: 40% of Delhi's PM2.5 year-round
5. Industrial and construction dust: 25-30% contribution

Time-of-Day Patterns (Delhi, October-February):
- 6:00–9:00 AM: Highest AQI — overnight accumulation + morning traffic + cold air
- 10:00 AM–12:00 PM: Improving — sun heats surface, breaks partial inversion
- 12:00–3:00 PM: Relatively best period — mixing layer rises, dilutes pollutants
- 4:00–7:00 PM: Rising again — evening traffic + cooling air retraps pollutants
- 8:00 PM–5:00 AM: Steadily worsening — no traffic reduction, cold air settles

Best air quality months for major Indian cities:
- Delhi: July–September (monsoon washes PM2.5; AQI typically 50–100)
- Mumbai: June–September (sea breeze + monsoon)
- Bengaluru: Relatively consistent; June–September best
- Chennai: October–December relatively worse due to northeast monsoon winds

=== SECTION 5: CITIZEN ADVISORIES FOR SEVERE AND VERY POOR AQI ===

CPCB and state PCBs issue the following citizen advisories during AQI > 300:

Mandatory measures:
- Do not venture outside unless absolutely necessary
- If going out: wear N95 (BIS-certified IS 9473) or N99 masks; surgical masks do not filter PM2.5
- Keep all windows and doors shut; use weather-stripping to seal gaps
- Run HEPA air purifiers continuously in living spaces and bedrooms
- Do not burn garbage, dried leaves, agarbatti (incense), or dhoop indoors
- Avoid cooking methods that generate smoke (deep frying, open flame)

Vehicle use:
- Use CNG, electric, or hybrid vehicles
- Use recirculation mode on car air conditioning to block outside air
- Avoid using two-wheelers — rider is directly exposed to road-level pollution (2-3x background)
- Carpool or use metro/bus to reduce road traffic

Health monitoring:
- Monitor for symptoms: persistent cough, breathlessness, chest tightness, eye irritation
- Asthma patients: carry rescue inhaler at all times; consider doubling preventer inhaler dose after consulting doctor
- If symptoms worsen: seek medical attention; air pollution can trigger cardiac and respiratory emergencies

=== SECTION 6: REAL-TIME MONITORING NETWORK ===

CPCB operates the National Ambient Air Quality Monitoring Programme (NAMP):
- 950+ monitoring stations across 344 cities (2023)
- Continuous monitoring stations: measure PM2.5, PM10, O3, NO2, SO2, CO in real time
- Data accessible at: https://app.cpcbccr.com/AQI_India/
- Mobile app: SAMEER (System of Air Quality and Weather Forecasting and Research)
  - Available on Android and iOS
  - Provides city-level and station-level AQI in real time
  - Issues health advisories based on current AQI category
