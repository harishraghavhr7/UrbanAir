SOURCE: WHO Air Quality Guidelines 2021
TOPIC: PM2.5 Health Effects and Guidelines
CATEGORY: health_guidelines

WHO revised its air quality guidelines in 2021, significantly tightening limits due to growing evidence of harm at lower concentrations.

PM2.5 Annual Mean Guideline: 5 µg/m³ (down from 10 µg/m³)
PM2.5 24-hour Mean Guideline: 15 µg/m³ (99th percentile)

Health effects of PM2.5 exposure:
- PM2.5 particles penetrate deep into the lungs and enter the bloodstream
- Short-term exposure causes respiratory inflammation, reduced lung function, aggravated asthma
- Long-term exposure is linked to cardiovascular disease, stroke, lung cancer, and premature death
- Children are especially vulnerable: PM2.5 affects lung development and can cause lifelong damage
- Elderly individuals and those with pre-existing conditions face disproportionate risks
- Even brief exposure to AQI >150 can trigger cardiac events in susceptible individuals
- PM2.5 crosses the blood-brain barrier, contributing to neurological effects

Protective measures endorsed by WHO:
- Stay indoors with windows closed during high-pollution events
- Use HEPA air purifiers to reduce indoor PM2.5 by 50-70%
- N95/N99 masks are effective at filtering PM2.5 when properly fitted
- Avoid outdoor exercise during pollution peaks (typically morning and evening rush hours)
- Indoor plants like snake plants and spider plants offer modest air purification
- Regular wet mopping reduces settled particulate matter indoors

SOURCE: WHO Air Quality Guidelines 2021
TOPIC: NO2 and Ozone Guidelines
CATEGORY: health_guidelines

Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2):
- WHO Annual Guideline: 10 µg/m³
- WHO 24-hour Guideline: 25 µg/m³
- Primary source: vehicle exhaust, power plants
- Health impact: Inflames airways, worsens asthma, increases respiratory infection risk
- Indoor sources: gas stoves, unvented heaters — ensure kitchen ventilation

Ground-level Ozone (O3):
- WHO 8-hour peak season guideline: 60 µg/m³
- Formed when NOx and VOCs react in sunlight — worst on hot, sunny afternoons
- Causes chest pain, coughing, throat irritation, worsens lung diseases
- Outdoor exercise should be avoided when ozone alerts are issued
- Ozone typically peaks between 2 PM and 7 PM — plan outdoor activities for mornings

SOURCE: US EPA AQI Technical Documentation
TOPIC: AQI Calculation and Health Breakpoints
CATEGORY: aqi_science

The Air Quality Index (AQI) is a standardized scale from 0 to 500:

0–50 (Good): Air quality is satisfactory. Little or no risk.
51–100 (Moderate): Acceptable. Unusually sensitive people should consider reducing prolonged outdoor exertion.
101–150 (Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups): People with lung disease, older adults, children should reduce prolonged outdoor exertion.
151–200 (Unhealthy): Everyone may begin to experience health effects. Sensitive groups: more serious effects.
201–300 (Very Unhealthy): Health alert. Everyone should avoid prolonged outdoor exertion.
301–500 (Hazardous): Health warning of emergency conditions. Entire population is at risk.

Berkeley Earth Cigarette Equivalence Study:
- 22 µg/m³ of annual PM2.5 exposure ≈ 1 cigarette/day
- AQI 150 (PM2.5 ~55 µg/m³) ≈ 2.5 cigarettes/day
- AQI 200 (PM2.5 ~100 µg/m³) ≈ 4.5 cigarettes/day
- AQI 300 (PM2.5 ~200 µg/m³) ≈ 9 cigarettes/day
- AQI 400+ (PM2.5 ~300+ µg/m³) ≈ 13-20 cigarettes/day

SOURCE: India CPCB (Central Pollution Control Board)
TOPIC: Indian AQI Standards and Winter Smog
CATEGORY: india_specific

India's National AQI uses the same 0-500 scale but is computed from 8 pollutants:
PM2.5, PM10, NO2, SO2, CO, O3, NH3, Pb

India-specific context:
- Delhi's winter AQI frequently exceeds 400 during November-January due to crop burning + cold air trapping pollutants
- Diwali firecrackers cause single-day AQI spikes to 999+ 
- Morning (6-10 AM) and evening (6-9 PM) rush hours have highest pollution levels due to traffic + temperature inversion
- Temperature inversion in winter traps pollutants close to ground, making mornings especially hazardous
- Indoor air quality in Indian urban homes can be 2-5x worse than outdoor due to cooking smoke, incense, and mosquito coils

CPCB Advisories for Very Poor / Severe AQI:
- Avoid outdoor physical activity entirely
- Keep car windows rolled up; use recirculation mode on AC
- Use N95 or higher masks outdoors — surgical masks do NOT protect against PM2.5
- Do not burn garbage, leaves, or agricultural waste
- Use CNG/electric vehicles or public transport to reduce your contribution
- Water spray on roads significantly reduces dust re-suspension

Emergency measures for AQI > 400:
- Schools should consider closure or move classes indoors with air purifiers
- Pregnant women, infants, and elderly should not go outdoors at all
- Athletes and manual workers should not perform strenuous outdoor activities

SOURCE: Indoor Air Quality Research - Multiple Studies
TOPIC: Indoor Air Quality Management
CATEGORY: indoor_quality

Indoor Air Quality Facts:
- Indoor PM2.5 is typically 60-80% of outdoor levels even with windows closed
- With open windows during heavy pollution: indoor levels can equal outdoor within 20 minutes
- Cooking (especially deep frying, high-heat) produces PM2.5 spikes up to 300 µg/m³ indoors

HEPA Air Purifiers:
- True HEPA filters capture 99.97% of particles ≥0.3 µm, including PM2.5
- Run on highest setting for 30 minutes to clear a room, then maintain on low
- Room coverage: 1 purifier per bedroom + 1 for living area
- Replace filters every 6-12 months depending on pollution levels
- DIY option: Box fan + HEPA furnace filter (Corsi-Rosenthal box) — 50-70% efficacy

Ventilation Strategies:
- Avoid opening windows from 6-10 AM and 6-9 PM (peak traffic pollution times)
- Best window-opening window: 11 AM - 2 PM when temperature inversion clears
- Cross-ventilation reduces indoor VOCs and CO2 — do briefly during low-AQI periods

Indoor Plants for Air Quality:
- Snake plant (Sansevieria): Absorbs NOx and VOCs, releases O2 at night
- Spider plant: Effective at removing formaldehyde and xylene
- Peace lily: Absorbs benzene, trichloroethylene, formaldehyde
- Note: Plants help marginally — they cannot match HEPA purifiers for PM2.5

Cooking and Indoor Pollution:
- Use exhaust fans / range hoods when cooking — reduces cooking PM2.5 by 60-70%
- Prefer cooking methods that generate less smoke: steaming, boiling over frying
- Avoid incense and mosquito coils indoors — they produce significant PM2.5
- Dhoop and agarbatti burning can raise indoor AQI equivalent to 200+ for 30 minutes

SOURCE: Nutrition and Environmental Medicine Research
TOPIC: Dietary and Lifestyle Interventions for Pollution Protection
CATEGORY: health_protection

Diet and Nutrition for Pollution Protection:
- Antioxidants neutralize free radicals generated by PM2.5 exposure in the lungs
- Vitamin C (citrus, bell peppers): 500 mg/day reduces PM2.5-induced oxidative stress by 20%
- Vitamin E (nuts, seeds, olive oil): Protects lung cell membranes from pollution damage
- Omega-3 fatty acids (fish, flaxseed): Reduce inflammation triggered by air pollution
- Turmeric (curcumin): Anti-inflammatory, shown to reduce PM2.5-induced lung damage in studies
- Green tea: Polyphenols protect against pollution-induced DNA damage
- Avoid heavy meat and processed food on high-AQI days — increases inflammatory response

Hydration:
- Drink 8-10 glasses of water on high-pollution days
- Water helps flush pollutants absorbed through the respiratory system
- Avoid alcohol on high-AQI days — dehydration worsens pollution effects

Exercise Adaptation:
- Avoid high-intensity outdoor exercise when AQI > 100
- Breathing rate during intense exercise is 10x resting — dramatically increases particle intake
- Opt for yoga, stretching, or indoor cardio on bad air days
- If outdoor exercise necessary: early morning tends to have lower ozone; use N95 mask
- Post-exercise nasal rinse with saline reduces inhaled particle burden

Children-Specific Protections:
- Children breathe 50% more air per body weight than adults
- School bags: include N95 mask (child-sized), extra water bottle
- School route: prefer walking routes away from main roads
- Avoid outdoor playtime during school recess on AQI > 150 days

SOURCE: Emergency Response Guidelines - Multiple Health Authorities
TOPIC: Vulnerable Population Protection
CATEGORY: vulnerable_groups

High-Risk Groups and Special Precautions:

PREGNANT WOMEN:
- PM2.5 crosses the placenta and is linked to preterm birth, low birth weight, and developmental issues
- Avoid all outdoor exposure when AQI > 150
- Use N95 mask if outdoor exposure unavoidable
- Ensure indoor air purifier runs continuously in living and sleeping areas
- Inform obstetrician of prolonged exposure for monitoring

INFANTS AND YOUNG CHILDREN (0-5 years):
- Never take infants outdoors on AQI > 150 days
- Use stroller cover/canopy that filters air when going out is unavoidable
- Ensure baby's sleep area has purified air (purifier in bedroom)
- Breast milk passes on some protective antioxidants — continue breastfeeding

ELDERLY (65+):
- Highest risk of cardiovascular events triggered by air pollution
- Monitor for unusual breathlessness, chest tightness — seek care promptly
- Keep emergency inhaler/medication accessible on bad air days
- Avoid morning walks until AQI < 100

ASTHMA/RESPIRATORY PATIENTS:
- Keep rescue inhaler always accessible during high-AQI periods
- Pre-treat with inhaler 15 min before any unavoidable outdoor exposure
- Track personal peak flow measurements — pollution can reduce lung function 10-20%
- Contact doctor if symptoms worsen — may need temporary steroid course

HEART DISEASE PATIENTS:
- PM2.5 can trigger arrhythmia and myocardial infarction — take AQI alerts seriously
- Avoid any outdoor exertion when AQI > 100
- Watch for unusual fatigue, chest discomfort, palpitations on high-AQI days
- Carry nitrates and emergency medications

SOURCE: Air Quality Monitoring Technology Guide
TOPIC: Understanding AQI Readings and Sources
CATEGORY: aqi_literacy

How to Interpret AQI Readings:
- AQI is calculated from the WORST pollutant at any given time — a single pollutant can drive the index
- Different monitors (IQAir, CPCB, WAQI, PurpleAir) may show different values due to different sensors
- PurpleAir and low-cost sensors tend to read 20-40% higher than reference-grade monitors
- Hyperlocal variation: AQI can differ by 50-100 points within 1 km (near roads vs parks)

Seasonal Patterns in Indian Cities:
- October-February: Worst pollution — cold air, temperature inversion, crop burning, Diwali
- March-May: Moderate — dust storms from Rajasthan, pre-monsoon heat
- June-September: Best air — monsoon washes pollutants; lowest PM2.5 of year
- Best cities for air quality: Lakshadweep, Mizoram, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh

Time of Day Patterns:
- 6-9 AM: High — temperature inversion traps overnight pollution + morning traffic
- 10 AM-12 PM: Improving — sun heats air, breaks inversion, disperses pollutants
- 12-3 PM: Usually best — mixing layer is highest, diluting pollutants
- 4-7 PM: Rising — afternoon traffic + cooling air traps pollutants again
- 8 PM-5 AM: High — overnight temperature drop + minimal wind dispersal
